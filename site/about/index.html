Moriarty Engineering is one person, me

I'm Chris Moriarty, a scientific software engineer in Boston.

Background

The dedication page of my master's thesis reads: “Dedicated to my Mom for letting my Dad buy the Apple IIGS instead of a car.” That decision shaped everything that followed. I was the neighborhood's IT kid before I could drive, dispatched by my mom to fix the PCs up and down our street.

I studied computer engineering at the University of Central Florida, then stayed for a master's in Intelligent Systems after an introductory AI course got its hooks in me. My thesis taught Quake II bots to play like the people they watched, which is still the most fun I've had with a research question.

After a few years building network test equipment and decision-support software, I found astronomy in 2012 and never left. Since then I've worked on Hubble and James Webb, HiCAT, a coronagraph slated for NASA's next flagship mission, the Submillimeter Array on the summit of Maunakea, and the Minor Planet Center, which keeps track of every known asteroid. Astronomers tend to specialize in one slice of the spectrum. I've drifted across infrared, visible, and submillimeter, and found that their software systems have far more in common than you'd expect. The field would benefit from being less siloed.

In 2024 I started Moriarty Engineering so I could choose the projects that interest me most. Most of my code is now written alongside coding agents, on open-weight models running on hardware I built, and my focus is making that way of working safe, efficient, and sound enough for science.

Open source
smax-pythonThe Python client for SMA-X, the Submillimeter Array's Redis-based real-time messaging layer. The SMA team still maintains it.
catkitThe Python control library for HiCAT, the Space Telescope Science Institute's coronagraph testbed. I started it in 2017, and the HiCAT team carries the work on in its successor, catkit2.
braidTurns a ticket into a pull request by running a coding agent through a graph of steps, each one checked deterministically instead of taken on the agent's word. OpenSpec and feature branching, with one interface to watch and steer both. MIT, in beta.
trogA self-hosted AI game studio. Give it a one-sentence idea, and a LangGraph state machine takes it through prototypes, milestone builds, alpha, and beta to a playable web game, writing the code and generating the art and music while people watch, steer, and judge. Apache 2.0.
trog-tooTrog rebuilt from scratch on a resident vLLM model (Qwen3.8-27B), and spec-driven from the first commit: every change starts as an OpenSpec proposal and is validated in CI. MIT.
freedokuAn offline-first sudoku app with no account and no server. Every puzzle has a unique solution and never needs a guess. Built with opencode on self-hosted models. MIT.
